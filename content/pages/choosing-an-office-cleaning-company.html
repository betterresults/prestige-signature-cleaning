Your office is often the first thing a client sees. When the cleaning is right, nobody notices. When it isn’t, everyone does. These seven questions will help you choose an office cleaning company you won’t have to chase.

1. Will you visit the office before giving a price?

Every office is different. The layout, how many people use it, the busiest areas and your access arrangements all change how long the work takes. A company that gives a fixed price without seeing the space is guessing.

A good cleaning company will offer a walkthrough first, then give you a price based on what your office actually needs.

2. Will I get a written cleaning specification?

A specification is a clear list of what gets cleaned and how often. It is the single best way to avoid misunderstandings. Ask to see it before you sign anything, and check it covers each area, for example reception, workspaces, the kitchen and washrooms.

3. Are your cleaners vetted and insured?

Your cleaners will often work when nobody else is in the building, so trust matters. Ask whether the team is DBS checked, how they are trained, and whether the company holds insurance.

4. How do you check the quality?

Standards can slip slowly over months. Ask how the company checks the work, how often, and what happens if something is missed. The answer should be simple and specific.

5. Who do I contact if something isn’t right?

You should have one point of contact who answers and puts things right. If every question goes into a general inbox, small problems can take weeks to fix.

6. How do you handle keys, alarms and access?

Ask how keys, alarm codes and building access are managed, and who holds them. A professional company will have agreed procedures and will be happy to explain them.

7. Can the cleaning fit around our working hours?

Cleaning should never get in the way of your team or your clients. Ask whether cleans can happen before or after hours, and how easy it is to change the schedule as your business changes.

Our tip: before the walkthrough, write down the two or three things that bother you most about the current cleaning. It helps the cleaning company focus on what matters to you from day one.
