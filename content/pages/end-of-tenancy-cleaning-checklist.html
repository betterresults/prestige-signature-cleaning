Moving out is stressful enough without worrying about your deposit. Cleaning is one of the most common reasons for deposit deductions, so it pays to know exactly what a letting agent or inventory clerk will look at before you hand back the keys.

How the check out works

At the start of most tenancies, an inventory is taken that records the condition of the property, room by room. When the tenancy ends, the letting agent, landlord or an inventory clerk compares the property with that inventory.

The aim is to return the property as clean as it was when you moved in. Normal fair wear and tear is expected, but dirt, grease and limescale that weren’t there at the start can lead to a cleaning charge. If you can, find your check in inventory before you start cleaning so you know the standard you are working to.

Room by room checklist

Kitchen

  • Inside and outside of all cupboards and drawers
  • Worktops, splashbacks and tiles degreased
  • Sink and taps descaled and polished
  • Hob and extractor hood cleaned
  • Outside of all appliances wiped
  • Skirting boards and light switches wiped
  • Bins emptied and cleaned
  • Floor vacuumed and mopped

Bathrooms

  • Bath, shower and screen descaled
  • Toilet cleaned and sanitised inside and out
  • Sink, taps and mirrors polished
  • Tiles and grout scrubbed
  • Cabinets cleaned inside and out
  • Extractor fan cover wiped
  • Skirting boards wiped and floor cleaned

Bedrooms

  • Dusting from top to bottom
  • Inside wardrobes and drawers
  • Skirting boards, doors and frames wiped
  • Light switches and sockets cleaned
  • Windowsills, internal glass and mirrors cleaned
  • Carpets vacuumed and hard floors mopped

Living areas

  • Dusting from top to bottom, including shelves and fitted furniture
  • Skirting boards, doors and frames wiped
  • Light switches and sockets cleaned
  • Windowsills and internal glass
  • Radiators wiped
  • Carpets vacuumed and hard floors mopped

The places people forget

Most deductions come from the same few places. Before you leave, check these again:

  • Inside cupboards, drawers and wardrobes. Crumbs and dust inside are easy to miss when everything is packed.
  • The extractor hood. Grease builds up on the underside and the filter cover.
  • Limescale. Taps, shower heads, screens and the base of the toilet.
  • Skirting boards and door frames. They collect dust in every room.
  • Behind and under furniture. Once the furniture is moved, the floor underneath is on show.

Our tip: leave cleaning until the property is empty. It is much quicker and more thorough when there are no boxes in the way, and nothing gets dirty again before the check out.
